## What the deck demonstrates

- **Slide 1:** a full-bleed park scene establishes place; a quiet translucent title field and overview remain readable.
- **Slide 2:** recurring park imagery creates identity while distinct panels communicate project, platform, solution, audience, scope, and grounding constraints.
- **Slide 3:** the pitch text is paired with a detailed ticket-journey visual that illustrates the fragmentation described in the copy.
- **Slide 4:** visitor needs are separated into meaningful audience groups, balanced by a central scene and supporting symbols.
- **Slide 5:** five distinct numbered illustrated tiles carry theme and recognition above an arrow-connected service process. A separate illustrated architecture explains routing, grounding, filtering and outputs; a calm guardrail list makes limits explicit. The [production example](production.md#worked-construction-example-tyy-slide-5) maps these regional jobs to coordinated asset creation and assembly.
- **Slide 6:** an interface screenshot and demo storyline show how the assistant can be demonstrated. They support a capability walkthrough, not measured real-world outcomes.
- **Slide 7:** supporting materials and resume information provide a practical close rather than decorative filler.

Together, the deck shows that a consistent identity does not require identical slide layouts: a recurring world and navigation system coexist with content-led structures. The concrete expression is strongly suited to this Ghibli Park project; the transferable standard is the relationship between context, message, structure, evidence, and readability.

## Apply the calibration

Choose the strongest relevant opportunities for this brief and identify their visible expression in the artifact. Explain a consequential omission by the audience benefit lost and the value of an alternative. The eleven principles are lenses for judgment, not an asset quota or a fixed template.

Evaluate what each choice establishes, clarifies, connects, supports or makes easier to read. Explicit plain-material requests and narrow edits can justify restraint; generic convenience does not justify bypassing useful visual meaning. Keep the source's particular imagery and identity tied to its intended use.
