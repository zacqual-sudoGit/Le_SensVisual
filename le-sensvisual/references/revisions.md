# Plain-language revisions

Users need not learn commands. Interpret the desired result, preserve required meaning and explicit constraints, and change the smallest useful scope. If the user requests broader redesign, reassess optional expressions rather than preserving them mechanically.

- **Quieter / less busy:** reduce competition and redundant detail while retaining hierarchy, contrast and the information needed for action. Retain illustrations that carry useful world, meaning or recognition; reduce redundant competition.
- **Bolder / more impact:** emphasise the central message or decision, without inventing metrics or enlarging irrelevant decoration.
- **More professional:** strengthen credibility, readable hierarchy and a coherent subject-specific visual identity for this audience.
- **Distill / shorter:** consolidate duplication and retain the key meaning and evidence. Reconsider once-useful visuals that are now redundant.
- **More immersive:** compose topic-relevant places, people, materials and experience while preserving calm reading space. Expressive scenery and incidental detail can build the world; distinguish them from factual claims about real facilities, products, routes or procedures.
- **Executive:** foreground the decision, evidence and next action. Keep the topic-specific expressions that help the audience grasp them.
- **Polish / typography:** correct hierarchy, spacing, type and collisions after checking intended content. Polish cannot rescue missing meaning.
- **Explain / diagram:** identify the relationship and what the audience needs to see. A drawing is useful when it improves recognition or understanding enough to justify it.

For a local edit or accepted-design transfer, preserve unaffected composition and artwork. For an authorized improvement, preserving function matters more than preserving every optional asset. Ask only when a material tradeoff remains unresolved; do not seek permission again for a change already requested. Inspect affected output and downstream delivery.
